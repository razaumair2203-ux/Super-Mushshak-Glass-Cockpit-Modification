# Sanitized Architecture Trade Study

A surviving early programme deck compares two candidate glass-cockpit approaches. The raw deck is **not published** because it contains internal evaluation language and detailed vendor architecture graphics. This page preserves the engineering method while removing internal/proprietary content.

## Decision criteria visible in the original work

| Criterion | Engineering question |
|---|---|
| Display architecture | How many displays are required, how are PFD/MFD functions allocated, and what happens under a display failure? |
| Cockpit layout | Does the arrangement preserve instructor/student visibility, symmetry and access to controls? |
| Engine display | Can engine/airframe parameters be integrated without compromising readability or maintainability? |
| NAV/COM integration | How well does the candidate integrate navigation/communication functions and existing equipment? |
| Synthetic vision / situational awareness | What training value is provided, and what is the display fidelity? |
| AOA / training cues | Which flight-training cues are available and how are they sensed? |
| Alerting | Are visual/aural warnings appropriate for an ab-initio training aircraft? |
| Third-party integration | Can the architecture accept non-vendor equipment and future upgrades? |
| OEM support | What engineering support, documentation and integration assistance are available? |
| Build quality / maintainability | How robust are controls, bezels, connectors and replaceable units in the training environment? |
| Cost | What is the acquisition/integration trade against capability and lifecycle support? |

## What this demonstrates

The modification was treated as a **systems trade study**, not a component purchase. A technically strong solution had to balance capability, training suitability, integration risk, lifecycle support, maintainability, cockpit ergonomics and cost.

## Public conclusion

Later public Super Mushshak literature confirms that the aircraft family was ultimately offered with both **Garmin G950** and **Dynon SkyView** glass-cockpit configurations. That is consistent with an architecture strategy in which customer needs, integration constraints and lifecycle considerations could drive configuration choice.

## What is deliberately omitted

The public portfolio does not reproduce:

- the original internal vendor ranking;
- detailed candidate wiring/system diagrams;
- internal price comparisons;
- vendor-specific support judgments;
- non-public part-number/configuration detail.

Those details are unnecessary to demonstrate systems-engineering depth.
