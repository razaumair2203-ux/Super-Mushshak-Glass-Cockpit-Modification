# Qatar Evaluation and Programme Context

## Customer evaluation

The surviving project archive provides unusually strong evidence for the customer-evaluation phase.

In September 2012, correspondence records the aircraft undergoing performance testing ahead of deployment to Qatar. During the subsequent evaluation in Doha, technical exchanges describe the aircraft flying **3–4 sorties per day** in hot/humid conditions while engineering issues were being fed back to the OEM.

By 12 November 2012, an internal status exchange records **10 evaluation sorties**, including a night-flying mission, with another night sortie planned.

The evaluation also exposed a key systems-engineering distinction: **functional performance is not the same as certification acceptability**. Questions around TSO/type-certification suitability became material to the customer's decision process even while the aircraft continued to demonstrate the avionics in flight.

## Later public programme outcome

Public reporting later documented a Qatar purchase agreement for Super Mushshak aircraft. Associated Press of Pakistan reported in June 2016 that PAC and the Qatar Emiri Air Force signed an agreement for supply of Super Mushshak aircraft, and in October 2016 reported an eight-aircraft supply deal.

Those later contracts establish programme-level context. This repository does **not** claim that one early prototype, one engineer or one evaluation event alone caused the sale.

Public sources:

- APP, 23 June 2016 — https://www.app.com.pk/national/pakistan-to-supply-super-mushshak-trainer-aircraft-to-qatari-air-force/
- APP, 22 October 2016 — https://www.app.com.pk/national/pakistan-inks-accord-to-supply-8-mushshak-aircraft-to-qatar/
- APP, 5 December 2016 — glass-cockpit Super Mushshak delivery to Nigeria: https://www.app.com.pk/national/pakistan-supplies-four-super-mushshak-to-naf/

The public export record demonstrates that glass-cockpit Super Mushshak configurations became part of the aircraft's later international programme, while the individual contribution claimed here remains limited to the documented early systems/integration, prototype, test and evaluation work.
